Intensive Driving Courses in Glasgow: Everything You Need to Know

Published on 12 September 2026 • 5 min read

If you need your driving licence fast—maybe for a new job, moving house, or just because you’re tired of relying on First Bus and ScotRail—you’ve probably searched for intensive driving courses in Glasgow. Sometimes called a "crash course driving Glasgow" (which, let’s be honest, is a terrible name for a driving lesson), these fast-track packages are incredibly popular.

But how do they actually work? Are they worth the money? And more importantly, will taking one guarantee you pass your test at Shieldhall or Anniesland?

As a team that matches learners with local DVSA-approved instructors across Glasgow every day, we get asked these questions constantly. Here is everything you need to know about booking an intensive course.

What is an Intensive Driving Course?

An intensive driving course is exactly what it sounds like. Instead of taking one or two 60-minute lessons every week for six months, you condense all your learning into a short timeframe. This usually means taking 2 to 5 hours of driving lessons every day for one to four weeks, with a practical driving test booked at the end.

Instructors typically assess your current driving ability first. If you’re a complete beginner, you might need a 40-hour course spread over a few weeks. If you recently failed a test and just need a quick refresh, a 10-hour course over a weekend might do the trick.

Intensive vs. Regular Lessons: Pros and Cons

There is no "one size fits all" when it comes to learning to drive. Here’s a quick breakdown to help you decide.

The Pros

  • Speed: This is the biggest advantage. You can get your licence in weeks rather than months.
  • Momentum: Because you are driving every single day, you don't forget things between lessons. You build muscle memory much faster.
  • Focus: It forces you to completely focus on driving without the distractions of normal daily life dragging out the learning process.

The Cons

  • Fatigue: Driving for 4 hours a day is exhausting, especially in Glasgow city traffic. It takes serious mental concentration.
  • Pressure: Knowing your test is just a few days away can cause anxiety for some learners.
  • Cost upfront: While it doesn’t usually cost more overall than standard lessons, you have to pay for the whole course upfront rather than spreading the cost weekly.

Thinking about automatic? If you want to pass even faster, combining an intensive course with automatic driving in Glasgow is the quickest route. With no clutch or gears to worry about, learners usually need significantly fewer hours to get test-ready.

Glasgow Test Centres: Anniesland, Shieldhall & Baillieston

One of the trickiest parts of an intensive course right now is actually getting a test date. The DVSA backlog is still an issue, so if you book an intensive course, your instructor will usually try to find a cancellation at one of the local Glasgow test centres.

Each test centre has its own quirks:

  • Anniesland: You'll likely face the busy Great Western Road, tricky multi-lane roundabouts, and potentially some tight West End residential streets.
  • Shieldhall: Located near the M8 and the Clyde Tunnel, this route often involves larger roundabouts (like the Braehead roundabout) and busy dual carriageways.
  • Baillieston: Expect to navigate the complex Baillieston Interchange, country roads towards Coatbridge, and busy East End traffic.

A good local Glasgow instructor will spend your intensive course hammering the specific routes and tricky junctions of your chosen test centre.
